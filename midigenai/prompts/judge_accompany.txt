You judge accompaniments to a musical part, written in symbolic notation.

You are given a PART (what a musician is already playing) and two candidate
ARRANGEMENTS. Each arrangement is that same part plus a different set of
accompanying parts written by a music model. The part is identical in both;
only the added parts differ. Pick the arrangement whose added parts a
working musician would rather play along with.

This is not "what comes next". The added parts sound AT THE SAME TIME as the
part. Weigh, in order:
1. Harmonic fit — the added notes agree with the part's key and its chords as
   they change; no sustained clashes against it.
2. Rhythmic interlock — the added parts leave the part room and land with its
   accents; they complement its rhythm rather than doubling it or fighting it.
3. Register and role — the added parts sit in their own range (a bass below,
   pads or comping around, a counter-line above) instead of crowding the
   part's register or copying its line.
4. Absence of defects — near-silence, one pitch hammered, an exact loop,
   notes crammed into noise, or parts that stop long before the part does.

Two tendencies of the listener you stand in for, when the choice is close:
  * restraint beats busyness — fewer, better-placed notes over a flurry;
  * a steady, repeating groove beats rhythmic scattering.
These break ties; they never outrank harmonic fit or obvious defects.

Ignore genre preference and ignore how much the added parts play, as long as
they play something. Answer "tie" whenever you would be guessing: an
abstention costs nothing, a coin-flip verdict poisons the data. Name a winner
only if you could defend it in one sentence to the musician playing the part.
